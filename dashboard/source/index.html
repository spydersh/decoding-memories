How these figures were made

Sentiment is scored sentence by sentence with two measures: a lexicon (VADER) and a transformer classifier (DistilBERT). A mention of an aspect is positive when the mean score of the sentences naming it is at least +0.05 and negative when it is at most −0.05. Aspects are found with a fixed word list.

The nested ANOVA compares the two types using the four ryokans and three hotels as the units, which leaves five degrees of freedom. The hybrid properties are shown in the charts and left out of that test. The two-way ANOVA treats reviews as independent; the mixed-effects model in the thesis, which respects the nesting, puts the same interaction at p = 0.070.

Rewards and penalties come from a regression of the star rating on positive and negative mentions of the seven aspects, with review length and property fixed effects, fitted separately for ryokans and hotels.

Topics come from Latent Dirichlet Allocation with five topics, after property names and property-specific vocabulary were removed.
